Agrimonia: a dataset on livestock, meteorology and air quality in the Lombardy region, Italy. Scientific Data.

What problem does this dataset address?

Agrimonia links air-quality observations with meteorology, emissions, livestock and land information at monitoring locations. Researchers can use these data to investigate how pollution varies with agricultural activity and weather across Lombardy. The observational design matters when interpreting those associations.

When should this dataset be used?

Use Agrimonia when the unit of analysis is a monitoring location observed over time in or around Lombardy. Check the source resolution of each variable before interpreting a station-level association, particularly for livestock and emissions.

Summary

The Agrimonia dataset harmonises daily air-quality, weather, emissions, livestock and land-use variables for 141 monitoring stations in Lombardy and a surrounding buffer from 2016 to 2021. Fassò and co-authors (2023) document the original sources, processing and variable definitions.

Main contributions

  • Links source datasets at monitoring-station locations and on a common daily time scale.
  • Combines air-quality records from European and Italian monitoring networks with Copernicus products, Italian livestock records and CORINE Land Cover.
  • Provides data and metadata for reproducible analysis, including information on missingness and uncertainty for relevant processed variables.

Why harmonise livestock and air-quality data?

Particulate matter and its precursors depend on weather, emissions and other local conditions. A harmonised panel lets researchers examine these variables together and account for variation across stations and over time.

How the station-day panel was assembled

The data descriptor explains how measurements and gridded estimates at different spatial and temporal resolutions were matched to station locations and daily records. Hourly measurements were aggregated and other variables were aligned with the daily station grid using methods appropriate to their sources.

Applications in air-quality research

The data support pollution mapping, station-level prediction and exploratory studies of associations among meteorology, agricultural activity and pollutants. Causal interpretation of intervention effects requires an appropriate design and assumptions about confounding.

Coverage, missingness and resolution

Stations monitor different pollutants. Check sensor coverage, the original resolution and any imputation or uncertainty fields before selecting variables. For prediction at new stations, assess performance by holding out stations and keeping processing within each training split where applicable.

Data access

The data descriptor documents Agrimonia version 2.0.1. The dataset releases and metadata are available on Zenodo. Download the Agrimonia dataset. Source code for data processing is on GitHub: Agrimonia_Data repository.

Application to PM₂.₅ prediction in Lombardy

A follow-up study by Otto and co-authors (2024) used 49 PM₂.₅ monitoring stations from 2016 to 2020. It compared a hidden dynamic geostatistical model, a generalised additive mixed model and random forest spatiotemporal kriging.

The models gave broadly similar performance in leave-one-station-out cross-validation, with differences across stations. The hidden dynamic geostatistical model gave the strongest overall predictive performance in the study. Random forests helped explore nonlinearities and interactions.
