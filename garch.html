Philipp Otto, Wolfgang Schmid (2023). A general framework for spatial GARCH models. Statistical Papers.

Summary

Spatial GARCH extends spatial ARCH by letting the scale at a location respond both to neighbouring squared shocks and to neighbouring scales. Otto and Schmid (2023) give a common framework for spatial and spatiotemporal ARCH and GARCH specifications. Different transformations and weight structures recover particular models, including spatial log-scale specifications and classical time-series GARCH.

The paper studies when the simultaneous system has a solution and develops nonlinear least squares for the general framework. Its simulation study examines finite-sample estimation accuracy. The Berlin application shows how spatial GARCH errors can describe local uncertainty in long-run condominium price changes after accounting for spatial dependence in the mean.

Shock propagation and scale feedback

Let \(Y\) denote the centred response or residual vector and \(h\) its squared local scales. The level-scale specification is

\[Y=\operatorname{diag}(h)^{1/2}\varepsilon,\qquad h=\alpha+W_1(Y\odot Y)+W_2h.\] The two matrices play different roles. \(W_1\) links observed shock magnitudes to the scale equation, while \(W_2\) links the scales themselves. This allows a scale change to propagate through intermediate locations, including locations whose own realised shock happens to be small.

Writing \(E=\operatorname{diag}(\varepsilon_i^2)\) yields \(h=(I-W_1E-W_2)^{-1}\alpha\) whenever the solution is well defined. The paper gives sufficient conditions for existence and positivity and shows how the choice of weights and innovation support affects those conditions. With oriented triangular matrices, the system can be solved in order. A log-scale formulation instead ensures positive scales through exponentiation. The nonlinear least-squares estimator uses log-squared observations and separates the strengths of the shock and scale terms for specified weight structures. Estimation error generally fell with increasing lattice size in the simulations.

Berlin condominium prices

The Berlin data contain one log price change per postcode area, calculated over 1995–2014 for 190 areas. The authors fitted a SAR mean, with and without covariates, and examined the resulting residuals. In the covariate model, the signed residuals showed little spatial autocorrelation, while absolute residuals still clustered (Moran's \(I=0.081\), \(p=0.018\), Section 5). The subsequent GARCH fit produced a map of geographically varying scales and little evidence of positive spatial clustering in squared standardised residuals.

The scale map describes where the fitted mean leaves more uncertainty about long-run price changes. Its interpretation is conditional on the spatial weights and mean specification. The application uses a spatial cross-section of long-run returns, so the results concern geographical variation over that interval.
