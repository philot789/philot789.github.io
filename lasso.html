Philipp Otto, Rick Steinert (2023). Estimation of the Spatial Weighting Matrix for Spatiotemporal Data under the Presence of Structural Breaks. Journal of Computational and Graphical Statistics. Open preprint.

What problem does this method address?

Otto and Steinert use a two-stage LASSO approach to estimate connections among locations while allowing for structural breaks in local mean levels. Accounting for these breaks matters because simultaneous changes in level can otherwise resemble dependence between locations.

When should this method be used?

Use the method when repeated observations are available for the same locations, the spatial weights are unknown, and mean levels may change during the observation period. Sparse connections are central to the penalised estimation strategy.

Summary

Otto and Steinert (2023) estimate a sparse spatial weights matrix and location-specific changes in mean levels from a panel of repeated observations. A first adaptive LASSO selects candidate break dates. A second constrained LASSO estimates spatial weights and retains changes in the local means. The paper illustrates the method with Berlin property prices.

Main contributions

  • Estimates the spatial weights matrix from the panel rather than fixing it from a geographical rule.
  • Allows unknown break dates that can differ by location.
  • Uses a constrained optimisation to estimate sparse weights and local mean changes jointly after screening candidate dates.

Why this approach is needed

Spatial autoregressive (SAR) and related models require a spatial weighting matrix \(W\). In practice, \(W\) is often assumed or selected from a small candidate set, which risks misspecification. This work proposes a data-driven method to estimate the full matrix \(W\) while also allowing for an unknown number of location-specific structural breaks in the mean.

Intuition

If means jump at different times across locations, those jumps can mimic spatial spillovers; conversely, strong spatial spillovers can look like simultaneous breaks. The estimator separates these by first over-detecting plausible change points in each local series, then letting the full spatiotemporal model retain only those needed while estimating \(W\).

Model

Observations at time \(t=1,\dots,T\) and locations \(s_1,\dots,s_n\):

\[ Y_t = W Y_t + a_t + \varepsilon_t,\quad \varepsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I_n),\qquad \text{with } \rho(W) < 1,\; \mathrm{diag}(W)=0,\; W\ge 0. \]

The vector \(a_t\) collects location-specific mean levels that may change at unknown times (structural breaks, not necessarily synchronized across locations). Reduced form:

\[ Y_t = (I - W)^{-1} a_t + (I - W)^{-1}\varepsilon_t. \]

Estimation (two-stage adaptive LASSO)

  1. Stage 1: change-point candidates (per location). For each location \(i\), fit a lower-triangular step design with adaptive LASSO to detect level changes in the overall mean trajectory \(\alpha_{t,i}\) (which already contains spillovers). Collect candidate change times \(\mathcal{T}^{(1)}_i\).
  2. Stage 2: joint estimation of \(W\) and local means. Stack all times/locations and solve a constrained LASSO: \[ \min_{\tilde a,\;\xi}\;\|Z\xi + \Phi \tilde a - y\|_2^2 + \lambda_b \|\xi\|_1, \] subject to \( \tilde a_{t,i}=0\) for \(t\notin \mathcal{T}^{(1)}_i\), \(w_{ii}=0\), \(w_{ij}\ge 0\), and \(\sum_j w_{ij}\le 1\) for all \(i\). Here \( \xi=\mathrm{vec}(W)\), \( \Phi\) maps change increments to levels, and \(Z = I_n \otimes Y\).

Penalty selection

Standard CV on prediction error can neglect spatial error correlation. The paper proposes selecting \(\lambda_b\) by matching the sample spatial autocorrelation (from mean-adjusted data) with the model-implied spatial autocorrelation (from \((I-W)^{-1}\varepsilon\)), e.g., via an MAE between correlation matrices. This targets the correct strength and pattern of \(W\).

Stability and interpretation

The structural spatial autoregression requires \(\rho(W)<1\) for the stable spatial multiplier. Nonnegative weights with row sums at most one give \(\rho(W)\le1\), so check the strict bound for fitted weights. A strict maximum row-sum bound is sufficient.

The fitted weights describe directed associations under the model. Use the orientation of the matrix and the spatial multiplier to interpret direct connections and the resulting network of indirect dependence.

Application to Berlin condominium prices

The case study uses monthly real-estate prices in Berlin postcode areas from 1995 to 2014. The method separates estimated changes in local mean levels from spatially transmitted departures and gives a sparse map of connections. Some fitted connections extend beyond immediate geographical neighbours.
