Philipp Otto, Rick Steinert (2023). Estimation of the Spatial Weighting Matrix for Spatiotemporal Data under the Presence of Structural Breaks. Journal of Computational and Graphical Statistics. Open preprint.

Summary

Spatial autoregressive models usually take a spatial weights matrix as given, often based on distance or shared boundaries. Otto and Steinert (2023) estimate that matrix from repeated observations of the same locations. Their model also lets each location's mean change at unknown times. This matters because an estimated link can absorb a shift in the mean, while a shift originating elsewhere can appear as a local break.

The paper proposes a two-stage LASSO estimator, a tuning criterion based on spatial correlation and conditions for parameter identification. Simulations reveal a practical limit: the procedure can recover many links and breaks, but it has difficulty determining direction when the true links are one-way. The application estimates a sparse network of connections among Berlin housing markets and separates it from changes in local price levels.

Local mean changes and spatial dependence

The model at time \(t\) is \(Y_t=WY_t+a_t+\varepsilon_t\), where \(a_t\) contains piecewise-constant local mean levels, \(W\) is shared across time and the innovations are assumed independent across locations and times. The observed mean is \((I-W)^{-1}a_t\). A change in one entry of \(a_t\) can therefore affect the observed mean at other locations even if their own local levels are unchanged.

In the first stage, the adaptive LASSO screens for possible breaks in each location's observed mean, using a step-function design. The second stage jointly fits the local level changes and \(W\), using a constrained LASSO over the weights and allowing level changes only at retained candidate dates. A break missed during screening cannot be recovered in the joint fit. The fitted weights have a zero diagonal, are nonnegative and have row sums bounded by one. Stability of the spatial multiplier requires the strict spectral-radius bound \(\rho(W)<1\).

Tuning and interpreting the fitted network

Instead of selecting the penalty by point prediction error, the paper compares the sample spatial correlation of the mean-adjusted observations with the correlation implied by the fitted spatial multiplier. The comparison targets whether the model reproduces the cross-location dependence. It is particularly relevant when the matrix is itself the object of interest. In the experiments, genuinely directed links sometimes appeared as pairs of reciprocal links. Individual edge directions therefore warrant caution, especially for small weights or short panels.

The Berlin application uses monthly condominium prices across postcode areas. Some fitted links joined areas beyond immediate neighbours, illustrating how a contiguity matrix can miss associations between geographically separated market segments. Such links are statistical associations under the assumed model. Omitted cross-correlated shocks or covariates can also contribute to the estimated weights.
