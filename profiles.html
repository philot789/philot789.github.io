Philipp Otto, Andreas Piter, Rik Gijsman (2021). Statistical analysis of beach profiles – A spatiotemporal functional approach. Coastal Engineering.

What problem does this method address?

The approach treats an elevation profile as a function of cross-shore position. It accommodates surveys that cover different portions of the beach and occur at irregular intervals. The Sylt case studies examine how profile evolution varies with storms and beach nourishment.

When should this method be used?

Use the functional approach when repeated coastal surveys record elevations along transects with unequal spacing, survey dates or offshore coverage. It is suited to questions about which parts of a profile change together.

Summary

Otto, Piter and Gijsman (2021) model sandy beach profiles as elevation curves indexed by cross-shore distance. Their functional spatiotemporal model accommodates incomplete surveys, describes dependence over observation times and alongshore locations, and supports interpolation and forecasting. Two studies on Sylt illustrate the method.

Main contributions

  • Represent irregularly sampled profiles as curves rather than requiring a common cross-shore observation grid.
  • Estimate spatiotemporal dependence and associations with external influences such as storms and beach nourishment.
  • Predict profiles between observed transects or at later survey times, conditional on available information.

Why this approach is needed

Monitoring sandy shorelines is critical for coastal management, yet beach profile data are often irregular, incomplete, and heterogeneous. Conventional analyses discard incomplete profiles and miss important temporal and spatial dependencies. This study introduces a functional spatiotemporal model that can handle such complexities and integrate external influences like storms and nourishments.

How the functional spatiotemporal model works

B-spline basis functions represent elevation over cross-shore distance. Fixed effects describe the mean profile and associations with external variables. Random components account for dependence across survey times and alongshore locations. Likelihood-based estimation uses the measured portions of the profiles.

Applications to submerged and emerged profiles on Sylt

Submerged profiles at Puan Klent. Estimated temporal dependence decreased in the offshore direction. The paper discusses how the results could inform the frequency of offshore surveys.

Emerged profiles at List. The estimated change following storms with wave heights above 5 m corresponded to a loss of about 0.2–0.7 m in elevation across parts of the profile. The analysis also examined changes associated with beach nourishment.

What the estimates can inform

The fitted model can help identify where repeated surveys add information and estimate how different parts of the profile respond to environmental events. Forecasting depends on available information about future conditions, and extrapolation to poorly sampled parts of a transect is more uncertain.
