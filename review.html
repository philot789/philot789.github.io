Philipp Otto, Osman Doğan, Süleyman Taşpınar, Wolfgang Schmid, Anil K. Bera (2025). Spatial and spatiotemporal volatility models: A review. Journal of Economic Surveys.

What problem does this review address?

The review distinguishes models that express local volatility through observed shocks and connected scales from models that introduce a latent volatility process. The distinction matters when choosing how to estimate the model and interpret a cluster of unusually variable observations.

When should this review be used?

Consult this review when choosing between a spatial ARCH or GARCH specification and a stochastic volatility model. The distinction is particularly relevant when a scientific question concerns dependence in variability across sites.

Summary

Otto, Doğan, Taşpınar, Schmid and Bera (2025) review spatial and spatiotemporal models for dependence in volatility. They connect time-series ARCH, GARCH and stochastic volatility to methods for sites, regions and networks, and compare their formulations, assumptions and estimation strategies.

Main contributions

  • Compares spatial ARCH and GARCH, log-scale formulations and latent stochastic volatility models.
  • Distinguishes contemporaneous spatial interactions from effects that propagate across time lags.
  • Discusses likelihood-based, moment-based and Bayesian estimation across model families.

Why model volatility as spatially dependent?

Connected locations can experience clusters of large residual magnitudes even when their signed residuals show little spatial autocorrelation. A volatility model describes dependence in their scale and can improve uncertainty quantification where that dependence matters.

Observation-driven and latent-volatility models

Spatial ARCH and GARCH use observed squared shocks and, in GARCH specifications, other scales as inputs to the scale equation. Spatial stochastic volatility models the volatility as a latent process with its own innovations. A latent process can evolve across locations and time even when observed shocks are small.

Contemporaneous and lagged interactions

Contemporaneous models link observations or scales from the same period, often through a simultaneous system. Lagged models describe how past shocks or scales at one site relate to current scales elsewhere. With simultaneous feedback, the scale need not equal the variance conditional on every other observation.

Applications of spatial volatility models

Potential applications include clusters of unexplained changes in local property prices, variability in environmental measurements and connected risks in financial networks. With repeated measurements, a model can represent geographic clustering and temporal persistence together.

Choosing a model for spatial volatility

Check whether squared or absolute residuals cluster after a plausible mean model. Compare alternative explanations, including omitted predictors and spatially varying measurement quality. Choose weights that correspond to the application, check stability, and compare fitted volatility or predictive intervals against the intended use.
